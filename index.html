04 — Dress code

Tropical formal

Linen suits, long dresses, real color. Dress for a beautiful evening outdoors in the tropics, not a ballroom.

Fabric

It will be around 29°C and humid at half past four. Linen, cotton voile, silk and seersucker will carry you through. Wool, polyester and anything lined will not.

Shoes

The ceremony is on clifftop stone and Rock Bar is built on the rocks at the water's edge. Block heels, flat sandals and loafers survive both. Stilettos lose on the first night.

06 — Questions

Things you might be wondering

The short answers. If yours isn't here, write to us.

When should I fly in?

Fly into Denpasar (DPS), Bali's international airport. Aim to land by Friday afternoon so you're at Rock Bar for sunset, and remember that from most places it's a long trip with a connection, so build in the time. If you can spare it, arriving Thursday gives you a day to find your feet.

AYANA is 25 minutes from the airport. Transport is on you, and it's easy: Gojek and Grab both work in Bali and pick up at the airport from the marked ride-hailing points, with a small airport surcharge. There's also a fixed-fare taxi counter in arrivals, and AYANA can book a private transfer if you'd rather have someone holding a sign.

Do I need a visa?

US passport holders can get a 30-day visa on arrival, either at the airport or online beforehand, and there's a small Bali tourist levy paid on entry. Your passport needs at least six months' validity past your arrival date and a blank page.

Rules change, so we'll send a reminder with the current requirements in the spring.

Where do I stay?

On the estate. Rooms are held for our guests across AYANA Resort, AYANA Villas and RIMBA, which share the same grounds, pools and shuttle. Once you've replied we'll send booking details and the group rate.

Do I need a car?

No. Everything happens on the estate, and the resort shuttle runs between the three hotels around the clock. The only ride you'll need is from the airport, and Gojek, Grab or a taxi will do it.

Can I bring a plus-one, or the kids?

Your invitation says exactly how many seats it admits, and the RSVP form only lets you reply for that many. The weekend is adults only, which we say more about just above.

What's the weather like in late August?

Dry season, and the best of it. Around 29°C in the afternoon, 24°C at night, humid, and very little chance of rain. The sun sets a little after six, which is why the ceremony is at half past four.

What should I wear?

Tropical formal. Linen, color, shoes that can handle stone and rock. The dress code section has swatches and the practical bits.

Is there a registry?

Getting yourself to Bali is the gift, and we mean that. If you'd still like to give something, we'll add details here closer to the date.

What about money?

Indonesia uses the rupiah. The resort takes cards for everything. A little cash is handy for taxis, markets and tips if you head off the estate, and the airport ATMs are the easiest place to get it.

Can I make a longer trip of it?

Please do. Sunday is wide open, and Bali rewards a few extra days. Uluwatu's cliffs and temple are twenty minutes south, and Ubud's rice terraces are a couple of hours north. We're happy to share what we've loved.

When do I need to reply by?

1 March 2027, using the form below. Earlier helps us hold the rooms.
